# The Problem

## Who this is for

Freelance designers, developers, writers, and consultants who:
- Bill 5–20 clients per month
- Pay $100–$300/month in subscription tools
- Have no accountant or finance team
- Have been ghosted by a client or a SaaS vendor at least once

## The two money problems

### Problem 1: The subscription leak

A freelancer pays for 8–15 tools (Adobe, Figma, Notion, Grammarly, ...).
Some stop working. Some double-charge. Some ghost the user after cancellation.

The user has no clean way to:
- See what they're actually paying for month over month
- Prove a charge was unauthorized or duplicated
- Build a dispute packet a bank will accept

### Problem 2: The invoice chase

The freelancer sends an invoice. The client says "I paid."
The money does not arrive.

The freelancer has to:
- Manually reconcile a bank statement against a client's claim
- Decide whether to follow up politely or escalate
- Find evidence if it becomes a dispute

## What they do today

- Squint at a bank statement PDF
- Search email for the invoice
- Guess whether the money arrived
- Write an awkward follow-up email

## Why existing tools don't solve it

- Personal finance apps (Mint, YNAB) categorize spending but don't verify claims
- Invoicing tools (Wave, FreshBooks) track what you billed, not what arrived
- Accounting software (QuickBooks) assumes a bookkeeper
- None of them answer the only question that matters: **"Did the money actually move, and can I prove it?"**

## What Trace does differently

Every claim — an invoice, a subscription charge, a client's "I paid" —
is checked against a real bank transaction. Every verdict comes with
evidence: the statement row, the timestamp, the source.

Nothing is confirmed without a matching bank entry.
